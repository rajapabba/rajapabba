### The thesis

Technology creates enterprise value when it improves economics, speeds execution, and strengthens operating performance. Deploying new systems doesn't do that on its own.

AI makes the gap sharper. Most enterprises now have AI investment; few have AI outcomes. The constraint is rarely the model. It's the missing business context, encoded judgment, and economic accountability needed to get from pilot to production. **AI is a capital allocation decision, not a software deployment.** Foundation models commoditize; encoded institutional judgment and governed unit economics compound.

My research and tools are organized around three questions:

| Discipline | Core question | Engineering focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **01. Value thesis** | What does a successful outcome cost in production, and what justifies the investment? | Unit economics, cost-to-outcome attribution, pilot-to-run-rate gates |
| **02. Expertise architecture** | Which expert decisions can be written down as rules, and which genuinely need agent reasoning? | Domain models, encoded decision rules, deterministic-vs-agentic routing |
| **03. Governed execution** | What has to be true before an automated action is allowed to execute? | Production gates, human approval points, audit trails, reversible actions |
